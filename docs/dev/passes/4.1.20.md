## Known limits, said as such

- **The trust root is the maintainer's software key**, `id_ed25519` (no passphrase), not the hardware key
  `id_ed25519_sk` the plan named (D37, 14 Oct 2026). A signature proves the key was used, not that the maintainer
  personally approved. Claude never writes an `approved` carry attestation, and the human yes is recorded explicitly.
  Accepted, blocks nothing; a rotation is its own pull request touching only `allowed_signers`.
- **Backup and restore hold the whole file in memory.** A restore peaks at about 1.4 to 3 GB for backups of 64 to
  256 MiB: about 10 MB of memory per MiB of backup, 20 MB for a restore. A 512 MiB container restores about 20 MiB.
  Measured by the candidate's `backup-memory` job; REALITY-OPS says the envelope; reducing it is 4.2's.
- **`bridge-load-sqlite` runs its three loads on one runner**, so one slow runner can read as a reproduced regression
  (×2.11 on #120 against ×0.78 on #121, the same code). A runner-speed probe or loads spread over runners is 4.2's.
- **`docs/dev/mutants.json` entries carry no line number**, and five contexts match several lines, so an entry also
  covers the same edit on its twin lines (a 4.2 follow-up).
- **No people's pass was made at the tag**, by design (D34): the thirteen `field-pass` issues (#75 to #87) run on the
  `4.2.0-rc` candidate. GO / NO-GO 4.2: NO-GO until they are made.
- **Nothing is published to npm**, and the four names are not reserved: a risk accepted until 4.2 (D34), watched by a
  nightly job that does not gate. `ship preflight-stable` lists what is missing.
